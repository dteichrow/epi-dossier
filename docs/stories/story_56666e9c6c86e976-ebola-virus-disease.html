What Matters Now

A short epidemiologic read of the active signal, bounded by what the monitor has actually captured.

  • Emergency declarations are coordination signals; epidemic size still depends on cleaned surveillance data.
  • Suspected and confirmed counts are moving on different clocks: illness recognition, testing, reporting, and retrospective linkage can all lag.
  • Urban or referral-hospital signals matter because transport, care seeking, and dense contact networks can change the response problem quickly.
  • Cross-border alerts and screening mark regional risk; sustained transmission outside the core outbreak area still needs source-level confirmation.
  • Healthcare-worker infections or deaths are an infection-prevention warning: the response system can become part of the transmission chain.

Historical Epidemiology Context

Urban Spread

Urban spread changes the outbreak problem: delays, care seeking, contact tracing, hospital exposure, rumor control, and institutional coordination all become harder at once.

Conflict-Zone Response

Conflict and insecurity distort surveillance and response: missed deaths, delayed samples, unsafe burials, interrupted contact follow-up, and distrust all change the denominator.

Suspected Vs Confirmed Counts

Early outbreak counts reflect case definitions, testing access, laboratory turnaround, and retrospective case finding. Suspected cases can rise before confirmation catches up, and cleaned totals can change after field investigation.

Location: DRC / Ituri

Ituri continues to account for the largest proportion of cumulative confirmed cases (68.9%) and accounted for 227 of the 442 cases reported in week 46 (51.4%), while Nord-Kivu (25.3% of cumulative cases; CFR 55.4%) accounted for 169 (38.2%). During epidemiological week 46 (9 – 15 November 2026), 442 confirmed cases and 239 confirmed deaths were reported, compared with 458 cases and 228 deaths in week 45; the national seven-day average has remained between 62 and 65 cases per day, and a new health zone, Watsa in Haut-Uélé, reported its first confirmed cases. Since External Situation Report #26, (data as of 7 November), an additional 512 confirmed cases and 270 confirmed deaths have been reported between 8 and 15 November, of which 442 cases and 239 deaths during epidemiological week 46 (9 – 15 November).
