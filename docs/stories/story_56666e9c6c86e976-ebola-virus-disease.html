What Matters Now

A short epidemiologic read of the active signal, bounded by what the monitor has actually captured.

  • Emergency declarations are coordination signals; epidemic size still depends on cleaned surveillance data.
  • Suspected and confirmed counts are moving on different clocks: illness recognition, testing, reporting, and retrospective linkage can all lag.
  • Cross-border alerts and screening mark regional risk; sustained transmission outside the core outbreak area still needs source-level confirmation.
  • Healthcare-worker infections or deaths are an infection-prevention warning: the response system can become part of the transmission chain.
  • Ituri and eastern-DRC signals require attention to access, security, transport, and laboratory-turnaround constraints alongside headline totals.

Historical Epidemiology Context

Conflict-Zone Response

Conflict and insecurity distort surveillance and response: missed deaths, delayed samples, unsafe burials, interrupted contact follow-up, and distrust all change the denominator.

Suspected Vs Confirmed Counts

Early outbreak counts reflect case definitions, testing access, laboratory turnaround, and retrospective case finding. Suspected cases can rise before confirmation catches up, and cleaned totals can change after field investigation.

BUNDIBUGYO VIRUS DISEASE OUTBREAK Democratic Republic of the Congo | Kenya | Nigeria - Weekly External Situation Report 25, Data as of 7 November 2026

Location: DRC / Ituri

Since External Situation Report #24, an additional 388 confirmed cases and 197 confirmed deaths have been reported, bringing the cumulative total to 10 920 confirmed cases, including 5 323 confirmed deaths [crude case fatality ratio (CFR) 48.7%]. Ituri continues to account for the largest proportion of cumulative confirmed cases (69.7%), although its relative contribution is declining, while Nord-Kivu (24.7% of cumulative cases; CFR 54.8%) accounted for 46.1% of the cases reported since the previous report, almost as many as Ituri (47.2%). The importation events in Kenya and Nigeria remain contained: no new case has been reported in Kenya since 19 October, six of the seven surviving patients have been discharged and the last contacts will complete monitoring on 11 November, while no secondary case has been identified in Nigeria, where contact monitoring is expected to end on 13 November.
